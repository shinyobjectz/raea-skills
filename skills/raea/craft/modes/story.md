# Mode: story

Films, trailers, music videos, brand films: pieces whose job is a feeling, and wanting to see more.

## It works when

The viewer feels something specific by the end and could say what the piece was about in a sentence. With a trailer,
they want the whole film.

## What to make

- **A world first.** The brief's `## Look` commits to one world: what each colour means, the light, the camera
  language, the type. Every shot belongs to it. Shots that look borrowed from different films break the spell.
- **A storyboard and a camera map before shooting.** For each shot: the subject, the framing, how the camera moves
  and how the cut joins the next shot (motion matched, a planned reversal, a hard cut on a hit). The person approves
  the board before anything renders.
- **Shots on a subject.** Close or medium on a person, an object, an action. A wide shot earns its place by showing
  where we are, once, and still has something in it.
- **Cameras moving through cuts.** A shot starts already moving and ends still moving, so the cut joins motion to
  motion. Easing to a stop and starting again reads as a slideshow.
- **Cut to the music's map.** Map the song first (`raea song FILE --plot`), then cut to its beats, downbeats,
  builds, stops and drops. Hold through quiet sections, cut faster through a build, hit the drop.
- **The narration is sparse.** Fewer, better lines with room around them. The picture carries what words would
  only repeat.
- **One image to remember.** Decide which frame is the poster and build toward it.

## The rut

A montage of pretty shots with no subject. Slow pushes on empty rooms. Every cut on the bar line. Grain, bloom and
lens flare doing the work the idea should do.

## Check

- The arc: does the energy build, breathe and land, or stay at one level?
- The world: cover the title. Is every shot clearly from this film?
- The ending: it lands on the image or the line the piece was built toward.
