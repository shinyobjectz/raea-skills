# Mode: explain

Walkthroughs, explainers, tutorials, product demos: pieces whose job is that someone can do or explain one thing
afterwards.

## It works when

The viewer could repeat the loop themselves: open it, do the thing, see the result. They don't need to have seen
everything, just one loop, all the way through.

## What to make

- **One project through one loop.** Pick a real project and follow it from the first step to the result. Each scene
  moves that project forward. A tour of features ("and here's the library, and here's settings") is the opposite.
- **Plan it before writing a line.** Who it's for and the one thing they should be able to do afterwards. Then a
  storyboard: scene · on screen · narration · payoff, one row a scene. The person approves the board before anything
  renders. Lines written to fit whatever is on screen read as random.
- **25 to 45 words a scene.** One step per scene. If the narration needs more, the scene is two scenes.
- **Say only what's on screen.** Every name the voice says is visible as it's said, and every thing the viewer has
  to find gets named. Time the camera and the cursor to the words, from the transcript.
- **A real cursor does the clicking.** It moves to the control, presses it, and the screen answers. Don't stand in
  for it with drawn outlines, boxes or spotlights.
- **Real screens, framed.** Use the real product at its real size, and move the camera to where the next thing
  happens just before it happens. Crop until the text that matters is readable. Never draw a fake screen.
- **Honest time.** Cut the waits and keep the result at real speed. Never show a speed the product doesn't have.
- **The payoff.** The last scene shows the result of the loop, the thing the viewer now knows how to make.

## The rut

Feature lists read aloud. A cursor wandering over a full-size recording. Device mock-ups floating on a gradient.
Narration that describes what the viewer can already see instead of why it matters.

## Check

- Pace: hold each step until its result has landed, then move on. A step that depends on a later one confuses
  everything after it.
- The voice: a calm, quick narrator. Faster usually reads better than slower, as long as the words stay clear.
- Critique it as a newcomer would see it: where do they lose the thread?
